# 1. 那通被挂掉三次的电话 / The Phone Call They Hung Up On Three Times

## 中文版

广州六月，天河大厦的冷气开得不够低，闷热的天气里，后背的汗把衬衫贴在椅背上。桌上摊着一张A4纸，圆珠笔划了二十几个名字，旁边是电话号码。有的名字后面打了勾，有的画了叉，大部分什么都没打。我刚拨完一个电话，对方听完第一句就挂了。那天第三个挂我电话的人。

那是2016年，我刚进租租车做市场合作。主要对接互联网公司、旅行社、境外旅游局。绝大多数时候都是线上畅聊，偶尔要给香港那边的合作方打电话。英语作为工作语言，开口前总要在脑子里过两遍，前几通手心出汗，话说到一半卡住，对方问"你是哪位"我差点答不上来。

桌上那张A4纸是我自己划的。每打完一个，就在名字后面做个记号。勾是约上了，叉是没戏，没打的是还没回复。一开始全是叉和空白，看着心慌。后来想明白了，叉不代表结束，过两周换个时间段再打。挂了就挂了，下一个。

加拿大旅游局的对接人，电话里回了三次"没空"。

第一次是下午三点，她说在开会。第二次是隔了两天，上午十点，她说最近忙。第三次我又隔了一周，她说还是没空。三通电话，每通不到三十秒。我把她的名字圈出来，旁边写了三个小叉。

第四次我换了个思路。不在工作时间打，挑了个周二下午快下班的时候。电话接通，我说我是租租车的，之前联系过几次。她愣了一下，说哦，是你啊。我说我知道您忙，不耽误您时间，就一分钟。她说你说。

我说我们做出境自驾，跟加拿大旅游局下半年的中国市场推广可能有结合点，想当面聊聊，方便的话我飞过去。

电话那头安静了两秒。她说，那你来吧。

我买了张机票飞去北京。她办公室在朝阳区一栋写字楼的高层，窗外能看见央视大楼。我在她办公室坐了一下午，聊的都是他们下半年在中国市场的推广计划，没怎么聊我们的产品。她讲他们的目标人群、投放节奏、合作渠道，我听着，偶尔插一句。

走的时候她说，你加我微信吧。

那天晚上我回广州的飞机上，靠着窗户想这件事。前面三次"没空"，不是拒绝，是我没说清楚我为什么要见她。第四次我说了"飞过去"三个字，她就答应了。她不是不愿意聊，是不愿意聊一个她不知道为什么要聊的事。

那张A4纸后来我一直留着。名字越划越密，勾也多了起来。有些人挂了我三次电话，第四次接了。有些人到现在都没接。

开口这件事，三年里慢慢磨出来的。磨的不是话术，是怎么在一分钟里让对方知道，你为什么值得她花这一分钟。

## English

It was June in Guangzhou. The AC at Tianhe Building wasn't low enough, the muggy heat pressing my shirt against the back of the chair. On the desk lay a sheet of A4 paper, twenty-some names scrawled in ballpoint, phone numbers beside them. Some names had ticks, some had crosses, most had nothing at all. I had just hung up from another call. The person on the other end heard my first sentence and hung up. The third hang-up of the day.

That was 2016. I had just joined Zuzuche doing market partnerships. My job was mainly to connect with internet companies, travel agencies, and overseas tourism boards. Most of the time, communication happened online. Occasionally I had to call partners in Hong Kong. English was the working language, and before I opened my mouth I'd run the sentence through my head twice. The first few calls, my palms sweated, words stuck halfway, the other person asked "who is this," I almost couldn't answer.

The A4 paper on the desk was mine. Every time I finished a call, I made a mark next to the name. A tick meant an appointment booked. A cross meant no chance. Nothing meant no reply yet. At first it was all crosses and blanks. I looked at it and panicked. Then I figured it out. A cross didn't mean the end. Two weeks later, try a different time slot. Hung up, fine. Next one.

The contact at Destination Canada said "I'm busy" three times on the phone.

The first time was three in the afternoon, she said she was in a meeting. The second time, two days later, ten in the morning, she said she'd been busy lately. The third time, after another week, still no time. Three calls, each under thirty seconds. I circled her name, drew three small crosses beside it.

The fourth time, I switched tactics. Not during working hours. I picked a Tuesday, late afternoon, just before she got off. The call connected. I said I was from Zuzuche, we'd spoken a few times. She paused, said oh, it's you. I said I know you're busy, I won't take long, just one minute. She said go ahead.

I said we do outbound self-drive, and there might be a fit with Destination Canada's China market push in the second half of the year. I'd like to talk in person. If it works, I'll fly over.

Two seconds of silence on the line. Then she said, fine, come.

I booked a ticket to Beijing. Her office was on a high floor of a building in Chaoyang, the CCTV Tower visible out the window. I sat in her office the whole afternoon. We talked about their China market plan for the second half of the year, barely touched on our product. She walked me through their target audience, their ad schedule, their partner channels. I listened, occasionally asked something.

When I left, she said, add me on WeChat.

That night on the flight back to Guangzhou, I leaned against the window and thought about it. The first three "I'm busy" weren't a refusal. They were because I hadn't told her why I wanted to meet. The fourth time, I said the words "fly over," and she agreed. She wasn't unwilling to talk. She was unwilling to talk about something when she didn't know why she was talking about it.

I kept that A4 sheet. The names got denser, more ticks appeared. Some people hung up on me three times, picked up the fourth. Some never picked up at all.

Starting a conversation — that, I learned, over three years, one call at a time. What I was grinding out wasn't a script. It was how to let the other person know, in one minute, why she should spend that minute on you.

# 2. 尖沙咀那间会议室的冷气 / The Air Conditioner in That Tsim Sha Tsui Meeting Room

## 中文版

2017年那个夏天，香港尖沙咀一栋写字楼的二十几层，冷气开得让人想加件外套。我坐在长桌一头，对面是亚洲万里通的人。亚洲万里通是国泰航空的里程计划，那年我们跟它谈租租车的合作。

冷气风口正对着我的后颈，我把杯子往旁边挪了挪。

会议室的玻璃墙外面是维多利亚港，船在海上慢慢走。我盯着看了几秒，把视线收回来。桌上一台我的笔记本电脑，一台他们的，我的方案已经打开在桌面。

我开始讲。我们做出境自驾，用户在国内租车，开到国外去。亚洲万里通的用户攒里程，里程可以换机票，但换完机票之后呢，落地了还得有人接他们去开车。我说，我们想做一个积分兑换的入口，里程可以换租车券，把你们在天上送的那段路，在地上接上。

对面那个戴眼镜的女生听完，没说话，低头翻我的方案。会议室安静了大概一分钟，只有冷气嗡嗡的声音。我手心有点潮，放在桌下擦了擦。

她抬头说，这个积分兑换的入口，我们可以做首个同类型平台合作伙伴。

我没让自己脸上太明显，点了下头。她又补了一句，说具体怎么落，要回去跟技术对一下。我说没问题，我整理一版对接文档发你。

会开了不到四十分钟。散会的时候她送我到电梯口，说下周给我回复。我说好。电梯门关上，我靠着轿厢壁，才发现后背的衣服是潮的。

出了写字楼，尖沙咀的太阳很毒。我从地铁口钻进去，站台上人很多，我找了个角落站着，掏出手机给同事发了条消息：成了，首个同类型平台合作。同事秒回一串感叹号。

后来我常想起那间会议室。冷气为什么开那么低，我没问过。但我记得那种冷，从后颈一路下来，一直凉到后背。也记得对面那个女生低头翻方案的那一分钟。那一分钟里，她没说话，我也没说话，但事情已经在动了。

有些合作不是谈出来的，是你把东西摆到对方面前，让她自己看见那个结合点。她看见的那一刻，你不用再说话。

那天下班我没直接回酒店，沿着尖沙咀海滨长廊走了一段。风从海上吹过来，带着一点咸味。维多利亚港的船还在走，比会议室里看的时候慢。我站在栏杆边，想这件事从打第一通电话到飞过来坐在这间会议室，前后花了两个月。

合作上线是三个月之后的事了。积分兑换入口上线那天，我在广州办公室，刷新页面看那个入口的样式，跟那间冷得让人想加件外套的会议室里讲的，一模一样。

## English

That summer of 2017, twenty-something floors up in a Tsim Sha Tsui office tower, the AC was cold enough to make you want a jacket. I sat at one end of a long table. Across from me, someone from Asia Miles. Asia Miles is Cathay Pacific's mileage program. That year, we were negotiating a partnership with them for Zuzuche.

The AC vent pointed straight at the back of my neck. I shifted my cup to the side.

Beyond the glass wall of the meeting room, Victoria Harbour, ships moving slowly across the water. I stared for a few seconds, then pulled my eyes back. On the table, my laptop, theirs, my deck already open on the desktop.

I started. We do outbound self-drive. Users rent cars in China, drive them overseas. Asia Miles users accrue miles, miles buy flights. But after the flight, when they land, someone still has to get them to the car. I said, we want to build a points redemption portal. Miles can be exchanged for rental vouchers. The stretch you fly them through the sky, we pick up on the ground.

The woman across from me, the one with glasses, said nothing. She looked down and flipped through my deck. The room went quiet for about a minute, only the hum of the AC. My palms were damp. I wiped them under the table.

She looked up. This points redemption portal, she said. We can do this. You'd be our first partner of this kind on the platform.

I didn't let it show on my face. I nodded once. She added that the specifics would need a check with their tech team. I said no problem, I'd put together a integration doc and send it over.

The meeting lasted under forty minutes. When it broke up, she walked me to the elevator and said she'd get back to me next week. I said okay. The elevator doors closed, I leaned against the cabin wall, and only then realized the back of my shirt was damp.

Out of the building, the Tsim Sha Tsui sun was harsh. I went down into the MTR, the platform was crowded, I found a corner, took out my phone and texted my colleague: Done. First platform partner of this kind. He replied instantly with a string of exclamation marks.

I thought about that meeting room often afterward. Why they kept the AC so low, I never asked. But I remember that cold, running down from the back of my neck all the way to my back. And I remember that one minute when she looked down at the deck. Neither of us spoke, but the thing was already moving.

Some partnerships aren't negotiated. You put the thing in front of the other person, let her see the fit herself. The moment she sees it, you don't need to say anything.

That evening after work I didn't go straight back to the hotel. I walked along the Tsim Sha Tsui promenade for a stretch. Wind off the sea, a little salt in it. The ships on Victoria Harbour were still moving, slower than they looked from the meeting room. I stood at the railing and thought about how long it had taken, from the first call to sitting in that room, two months end to end.

The partnership went live three months later. The day the points portal launched, I was in our Guangzhou office, refreshing the page to look at the portal's layout. Exactly the same as what we'd talked about in that meeting room, the one where the AC made you want to put on a jacket.

# 3. 手心出汗的第一天 / Sweaty Palms on the First Day

## 中文版

2022年十月，我从太平洋汽车辞职，跟Flora合伙做企鹅探学。第一天上班，我坐在广州越秀区一个共享办公的工位上，月租一千出头。面前是一个空的Notion页面，光标在闪。

公司只有我一个人。

Flora在广州另一头，她手头还有自己的事，隔天来一次。我那天的任务是自己想清楚，我们要做什么。亲子研学，这四个字写在Notion页面的标题栏，下面什么都没有。

我盯着光标看了大概十分钟，手心开始出汗。

之前在租租车、易车、太平洋汽车，我都是带着团队、带着品牌、带着资源去做事。别人听你说话，很大一部分是在听你身后那个logo。开口之前，信任已经有一半了。那天坐在共享办公里，我身后什么都没有。没有logo，没有团队，没有名片上的公司名。只有我自己。

下午两点，我去茶水间倒水。隔壁工位一个男生抬头看了我一眼，问我是做什么的。我说亲子研学。他说哦，然后低头继续干活。我端着水杯回工位，那两个字说出口的时候，我自己都觉得空。研学，研什么学，给谁研，怎么研，我一个字都答不上来。

那天晚上Flora来找我，问我第一天怎么样。我说Notion页面还是空的。她笑了一下，说没关系，第一天都这样。

我说不是空的问题，是我发现我以前做的那些事，都是别人把框架搭好我去填。现在框架要我自己搭，我不知道从哪根线开始。

她说那你先别想框架，先想一个问题，你为什么要做这件事。

我想了很久。为什么做亲子研学。我在太平洋汽车的时候，做车主会员体系，把人从一个页面送到另一个页面。再早一点在租租车，把人从一个国家送到另一个国家。我一直在做的事情，其实是把人从一个地方送到另一个地方。亲子研学，是把孩子从城市送到田里，把田里的东西带回家。

那天晚上我在共享办公待到十一点。前台小哥来问我走不走，他要锁门。我说我再待五分钟。他走了之后那五分钟里，我在Notion页面上写下第一行字：把城里的孩子送到田里。

第二天开始，事情慢慢动起来。产品形态、第一批基地、第一批导师、第一批用户怎么来，一个一个写。每一个写下来之前都要想很久，写下来之后发现也没那么难。难的是第一行。

第一个基地跑了六七趟南沙才谈下来。第一次去，对方根本没听我说完，直接说我们不跟机构合作。第二次去，我带了个方案，他看了两眼说再说。第三次去，我带了一箱水果和一摞打印的案例。第四次、第五次、第六次。第七次去的时候，他看完我说的话，问了一句，你们自己有场地吗。我说有。他说那聊聊。

签下来的那天晚上，我在客厅坐了很久。茶几上堆着电脑和外卖盒，窗外广州的夜景一片一片亮着。从零搭东西，最难的是开始那一下。你不知道搭出来的有没有人要。

那天的手心出汗，后来我经常想起来。不是紧张那种出汗，是那种你站在一个空房间中央、四面都是白的、你不知道往哪走的那种出汗。

后来我知道了，往哪走都行，先走一步。第一步走出去，房间就不是空的了。

## English

In October 2022, I quit Pacific Auto and partnered with Flora to build Penguin Explore. First day at work, I sat at a desk in a co-working space in Yuexiu, Guangzhou, rent just over a thousand a month. In front of me, an empty Notion page, cursor blinking.

The company was me. Just me.

Flora was on the other side of Guangzhou, still had her own things, came by every other day. My job that day was to figure out, by myself, what we were going to do. Parent-child study tours. Those four words sat in the Notion page's title bar, nothing below them.

I stared at the cursor for about ten minutes. My palms started sweating.

Before this, at Zuzuche, BitAuto, Pacific Auto, I'd always worked with a team, a brand, resources behind me. When people listened to me, half of what they were listening to was the logo behind me. Before I opened my mouth, half the trust was already there. That day in the co-working space, there was nothing behind me. No logo, no team, no company name on a business card. Just me.

At two in the afternoon, I went to the pantry to get water. A guy at the next desk looked up, asked what I did. Parent-child study tours, I said. Oh, he said, and went back to work. I carried the cup back to my desk. When those words came out of my mouth, even I felt how empty they sounded. Study tours. Study what, for whom, how. I couldn't answer a single one.

That night Flora came by, asked how the first day went. I said the Notion page was still empty. She laughed. Said it's fine, every first day is like that.

I said it's not about being empty. It's that I realized everything I'd done before, someone else had built the frame and I went and filled it. Now the frame was on me, and I didn't know which thread to start with.

She said then don't think about the frame. Think about one question first. Why are you doing this.

I thought about it a long time. Why parent-child study tours. At Pacific Auto, I'd built a car-owner membership system, moving people from one page to another. Earlier at Zuzuche, moving people from one country to another. What I'd been doing all along was moving people from one place to another. Parent-child study tours, that was moving kids from the city to the fields, and bringing what was in the fields back home.

That night I stayed in the co-working space until eleven. The guy at the front desk came over and asked if I was leaving, he had to lock up. I said five more minutes. After he left, in those five minutes, I wrote the first line on the Notion page: take the city kids to the fields.

From the next day on, things started moving. Product form, first batch of bases, first mentors, how the first users would come, written down one by one. Before each one was written, a lot of thinking. After it was written, it turned out not to be that hard. The hard part was the first line.

The first base took six or seven trips to Nansha before it was signed. First time, the other side didn't even let me finish, said straight out we don't work with agencies. Second time, I brought a deck, he glanced at it, said we'll see. Third time, I brought a crate of fruit and a stack of printed cases. Fourth, fifth, sixth. The seventh time, after he listened to me, he asked, do you have your own site. I said yes. He said, let's talk.

The night we signed, I sat in the living room a long time. The coffee table was stacked with a laptop and takeout boxes, outside the window Guangzhou's lights coming on piece by piece. Building something from zero, the hardest part is the first move. You don't know if what you're building, anyone will want.

The sweaty palms that day, I've thought about them a lot since. Not nerves. The kind of sweat when you stand in the middle of an empty room, four white walls, you don't know which way to walk.

Later I learned, any direction works, just take one step. After the first step, the room isn't empty anymore.

# 4. 单程四十分钟 / Forty Minutes Each Way

## 中文版

长沙早高峰，2号线转6号线，单程四十分钟。我每天七点四十从万家丽广场进站，车厢挤得转不开身，有人低头刷手机，有人闭着眼靠在车门上。我习惯站在车厢连接处，手扶着那根冰凉的杆子。到迎宾路口换乘6号线，再坐几站。

这四十分钟一般放空。但有时候脑子自己转起来，转到过去某个瞬间，或者转到未来某个不确定的地方。

前几天早上，车过万家丽广场的时候，我突然想起2016年。那年我刚进租租车，第一次拿起电话打给境外旅游局。手心出汗，话说到一半卡住。后来打了三年电话，从挂三次到约上，从约上到飞过去见面。那三年我学到的不是话术，是怎么在一分钟里让对方知道你为什么值得这一分钟。

换乘的时候跟着人流走，6号线的站台人也比平时多。

2022年那个夏天我又想起来过一次。那年我从太平洋汽车辞职，跟Flora做企鹅探学。辞职那天上午开了两个会，会上有人说接下来要把活跃度做到百分之七十。我在会议室里听着，那个数字跟我没关系了。散会回工位，打开邮箱写辞职信，写得很慢，删了改，改了删，最后只剩一句"本人因个人原因提出离职"。

那段时间我每天从天河的家坐地铁去越秀区的办公室，单程四十分钟。那时候是广州地铁，现在是长沙地铁，时间一样长。那四十分钟里我经常想一件事，我从2016年打第一通电话开始，到2022年自己开公司，中间隔了六年。六年里换了三个公司，做了五六个项目，从市场合作做到会员体系做到用户运营。每一份工作看起来不一样，拆开看其实都在做同一件事，把人从一个地方送到另一个地方。

地铁报站，6号线到了下一站。我松开扶着杆子的手，手心有点凉。

2025年我回了长沙。白天上班，晚上偶尔去酒吧。酒吧叫葡滋，跟一个姓周的兄弟合伙开的。决定开酒吧那天是在他家里喝酒，喝到半夜，他说要不我们一起开个酒吧。我当时有点上头，说行。第二天醒来想了想，觉得也行。

白天上班有白天的节奏，晚上酒吧有晚上的节奏，两件事搭在一起，人反而比只做一件事的时候清醒。

我有时候在吧台后面站着，看一个客人点一杯酒，喝两口放下，跟同伴说句什么，两个人都笑了。我会忽然反应过来，从2016年到现在，做了快十年，我一直在学怎么把东西做出来、推出去。回到长沙之后慢慢学会的，是停下来看看做出来的东西有没有人真在用。

地铁又报站了。我抬起头，看了眼站名。

我扶着杆子，看着车窗外的隧道灯一盏一盏往后退。过去十年，从广州到长沙，从打电话到开酒吧，每一段路其实都像这四十分钟，挤、闷、转不开身，但站够了时间，就到了。

长丰站到了。我松开手，往车门那边走。

出地铁口，扫一辆共享单车。长沙早上八点多的太阳已经有热气，骑出去没几分钟，路边早餐店的蒸气飘过来，混着油条和豆浆的味道。

骑过一段长上坡，十五分钟后到公司楼下。锁车，进电梯，按下楼层。

## English

Changsha morning rush. Line 2 to Line 6, forty minutes each way. I enter at Wanjiali Square every morning at seven forty. The carriage is packed, can't turn around. Someone's head down scrolling, someone's eyes closed leaning against the door. I always stand in the gangway between carriages, hand on that cold metal pole. Transfer to Line 6 at Yingbin Road, a few stops more.

Those forty minutes I usually blank out. But sometimes my mind starts turning on its own, lands on some moment in the past, or some uncertain place in the future.

The other morning, the train pulling away from Wanjiali Square, I suddenly thought of 2016. That year I'd just joined Zuzuche, picked up the phone for the first time to call an overseas tourism board. Palms sweating, words stuck halfway. After three years of calls, from being hung up on three times to booking a meeting, from booking to flying out to see them in person. What I learned in those three years wasn't a script. It was how to let the other person know, in one minute, why you were worth that minute.

Transferring, following the flow of people. The Line 6 platform was busier than usual too.

The summer of 2022 came back to me once too. That year I quit Pacific Auto, started Penguin Explore with Flora. The morning of the day I resigned, two meetings. In one, someone said next we need to push engagement to seventy percent. I sat in the meeting room listening, that number had nothing to do with me anymore. After the meeting, back at my desk, I opened my email and started the resignation letter. Typed slow, deleted, retyped, deleted, in the end left just one line, "I am resigning for personal reasons."

That period, every day I took the subway from my place in Tianhe to the office in Yuexiu, forty minutes each way. Guangzhou subway then, Changsha subway now, same length of time. In those forty minutes I used to think about one thing. From that first call in 2016 to opening my own company in 2022, six years in between. Six years, three companies, five or six projects, from market partnerships to membership systems to user ops. Each job looked different on the surface. Taken apart, all the same thing. Moving people from one place to another.

The train announcement, next stop on Line 6. I let go of the pole. My palm was cool.

In 2025 I moved back to Changsha. Daytime, work. Nighttime, sometimes the bar. The bar is called Puzi, opened with a guy named Zhou. The decision to open it was made at his place one night, drinking, into the small hours, when he said let's open a bar together. I was a bit drunk, said fine. Woke up the next day, thought about it, decided fine.

Daytime work has its rhythm, nighttime bar has its rhythm. The two together, somehow you stay more clear-headed than when you only do one.

Sometimes I stand behind the bar, watch a customer order a drink, take two sips, put it down, say something to the friend he's with, both of them laugh. And I suddenly realize, from 2016 to now, almost ten years, I've been learning how to make things and push them out. After moving back to Changsha, what I've slowly been learning is to stop and look, whether the things I've made, anyone is actually using.

The announcement again. I looked up, checked the station name.

I hold the pole, watch the tunnel lights outside the window slip past one by one. The past ten years, from Guangzhou to Changsha, from cold calls to running a bar, every stretch of road has been like these forty minutes. Crowded, stuffy, can't turn around. But if you stand there long enough, you arrive.

Changfeng station. I let go of the pole and walk toward the door.

Out of the station, scan a shared bike. The Changsha sun at eight-something in the morning already has heat in it. A few minutes in, steam from a breakfast shop drifting across the road, the smell of youtiao and soy milk.

A long uphill stretch, fifteen minutes later at the building. Lock the bike, into the elevator, press the floor.
