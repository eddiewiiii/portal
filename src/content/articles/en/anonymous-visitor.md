The little black cat downstairs brushed against my trouser leg, then walked off.

I found the animal interesting. Also felt, somehow, that there was something between us. Then the thought started: I wanted to take it in.

It was always downstairs, a dark lump, didn't look like anyone was looking after it. I'd go in and out every day; sometimes it was there, sometimes not. A few times I came home and it saw me—didn't hide, just looked up.

I thought, this little thing, if I could bring it home, give it a place to eat and sleep, that'd work. Hard to say why.

People say stray cats are afraid of people. This one came over to brush against me. That single rub, I think, was what moved me.

I went over it in my head a few times. Where it would sleep. Whether it'd be loud at night. Who'd feed it when I was out. None of it was real yet, but I thought about it seriously—so seriously it was as if it was already home.

Then it was gone. First couple of days I didn't see it, figured it was out roaming. A few more days, still nothing.

The corner downstairs was empty. The spot where it used to sit, evening light falling on it, not a hair left.

When I pass by, I still take an extra look. I know it's gone, but my feet slow half a beat, my eyes drift that way.

The first time I noticed the empty spot, I stood for a moment, then walked on. After that, every time the same. One look, walk on.

Sometimes that's how these things go. It comes, you don't think much of it, it brushes against you, you think it's nice. By the time you want to keep it, give it a home, it's already gone. Right after you got serious. Gone.

Not sad, exactly. It came. Brushed against my trouser leg. Stayed in that corner downstairs. Like an unnamed visitor—stopped by once, then left.

Today I passed downstairs again. Still took a look.

Empty.
