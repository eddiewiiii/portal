It was June in Guangzhou. The AC at Tianhe Building wasn't low enough, the muggy heat pressing my shirt against the back of the chair. On the desk lay a sheet of A4 paper, twenty-some names scrawled in ballpoint, phone numbers beside them. Some names had ticks, some had crosses, most had nothing at all. I had just hung up from another call. The person on the other end heard my first sentence and hung up. The third hang-up of the day.

That was 2016. I had just joined Zuzuche doing market partnerships. My job was mainly to connect with internet companies, travel agencies, and overseas tourism boards. Most of the time, communication happened online. Occasionally I had to call partners in Hong Kong. English was the working language, and before I opened my mouth I'd run the sentence through my head twice. The first few calls, my palms sweated, words stuck halfway, the other person asked "who is this," I almost couldn't answer.

The A4 paper on the desk was mine. Every time I finished a call, I made a mark next to the name. A tick meant an appointment booked. A cross meant no chance. Nothing meant no reply yet. At first it was all crosses and blanks. I looked at it and panicked. Then I figured it out. A cross didn't mean the end. Two weeks later, try a different time slot. Hung up, fine. Next one.

The contact at Destination Canada said "I'm busy" three times on the phone.

The first time was three in the afternoon, she said she was in a meeting. The second time, two days later, ten in the morning, she said she'd been busy lately. The third time, after another week, still no time. Three calls, each under thirty seconds. I circled her name, drew three small crosses beside it.

The fourth time, I switched tactics. Not during working hours. I picked a Tuesday, late afternoon, just before she got off. The call connected. I said I was from Zuzuche, we'd spoken a few times. She paused, said oh, it's you. I said I know you're busy, I won't take long, just one minute. She said go ahead.

I said we do outbound self-drive, and there might be a fit with Destination Canada's China market push in the second half of the year. I'd like to talk in person. If it works, I'll fly over.

Two seconds of silence on the line. Then she said, fine, come.

I booked a ticket to Beijing. Her office was on a high floor of a building in Chaoyang, the CCTV Tower visible out the window. I sat in her office the whole afternoon. We talked about their China market plan for the second half of the year, barely touched on our product. She walked me through their target audience, their ad schedule, their partner channels. I listened, occasionally asked something.

When I left, she said, add me on WeChat.

That night on the flight back to Guangzhou, I leaned against the window and thought about it. The first three "I'm busy" weren't a refusal. They were because I hadn't told her why I wanted to meet. The fourth time, I said the words "fly over," and she agreed. She wasn't unwilling to talk. She was unwilling to talk about something when she didn't know why she was talking about it.

I kept that A4 sheet. The names got denser, more ticks appeared. Some people hung up on me three times, picked up the fourth. Some never picked up at all.

Starting a conversation — that, I learned, over three years, one call at a time. What I was grinding out wasn't a script. It was how to let the other person know, in one minute, why she should spend that minute on you.
