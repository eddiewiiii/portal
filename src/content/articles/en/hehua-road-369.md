Last night, a delivery guy was waiting at my door.

A bag of grapes, a couple pounds. The guy said that he was told to handle with care.

I brought it inside and opened the bag. Kyoho grapes, big ones, skin tight, stems still green. Popped one in my mouth, it was sweet.

The kind of sweet that comes straight off the vine, juice full, it splashes when you bite through.

My friend XiaoHai messaged me: I went to Ningxiang today to pick these, thought you'd like a taste.

Ningxiang is fifty or sixty kilometers from Changsha city. He drove out, picked a bag of grapes, then spent a few dozen yuan on a delivery runner to bring them to me.

I guess the delivery cost more than the grapes.

I replied okay and thanks.

We had noodles together earlier that day. He had a list of noodle shops saved up, taking me through them one by one. One was tucked in an old residential alley in Xinkaipu. Small storefront, a few tables, the owner rolling dough herself.

He ordered me a beef noodle soup and got himself one with shredded pork. The bowl came out with clear broth, scallions floating on top. Took a bite. The noodles had bounce, the broth was clean and savory.

He sat across watching me eat, asked how it was.

I said good. He then smiled, said it took him a long time to find this little treasure hidden inside a residential building.

After noodles he took me to get braised snacks. The shop that I like, near his apartment. He bought duck necks, lotus root slices, kelp knots, packed them in a bag and handed it to me.

This was the second time.

Last Sunday he'd brought a box too. He'd been over that way running errands, picked them up on the way back. Pulled up to my building, passed the bag through the car window and the box was still warm.

Every time I came back to Changsha, he was the first one to call.

Ask if I'd arrived, and then take me out to eat.

I moved back to Changsha last year. He was even happier about it than I was.

Sometimes he brings something, sometimes he takes me to a good spot he found.

Earlier this month, he drove over with two big bags of vegetables. His little bichon was in the passenger seat, poking its head out to look at me, so fat it curled into a ball.

Two bags stuffed full, all from his family's own garden, washed clean. He clapped my shoulder and talked for a while. We stood downstairs and chatted.

I carried the bags upstairs. Before putting them in the fridge I squatted on the kitchen floor, opening them one by one. Pulled out a cucumber, rinsed it under the tap, took a bite.

Sweet.

And I took another bite to make sure.

That earthy sweetness, solid, a crisp sound when you chew. Juice ran down the corner of my mouth, tongue went cold first, then the sweetness came back.

Messaged him the cucumber is really good.

He replied fast. Said farm vegetables don't look great but they taste good, the corn might be a bit tough, but that's how homegrown is.

Eighteen kilometers, 11 p.m., 369 Hehua Road. Someone carried two big bags of fresh vegetables upstairs, happy.

My mom called me when I stepped in and asked how I've been. I said good, a friend keeps coming around. She asked, is it Xiaohai? I said yeah. She said XiaoHai is the kind of friend you keep for life, and I should appreciate that.

I said, I know.

Last night I washed a bunch of grapes, put them in a bowl, brought them to my desk.

Still sweet.
