Day one on the job, the boss handed me a task: in thirty days, build the entire brand system for Xiao A Fairy, an AI English tutoring product for kids.

Positioning. Visual system. Brand manual. Website. Launch event. Everything.

I sat at my desk, stared at that sentence for a long time. Thirty days for positioning, visual, site, event. Short on time, short on people, high bar. I thought to myself, okay then.

Day three, late at night, I was buried in competitor decks and user feedback, and I got stuck. The past two days, I'd been thinking what do we make — AI teaching, smart tutoring, personalized learning, listed a dozen directions, each looked right, each also felt vague. That night, I wrote every direction on the whiteboard, stared at it for half an hour, and suddenly saw where the problem was.

I had been adding things. I had not been subtracting.

Next day I switched approaches. Stopped asking what to build. Started asking what not to build.

I sat with the founder for a week straight, an hour a day, went through every accumulated user comment, every competitor slide, line by line. On the fourth day, he said something offhand: "Parents don't actually pick us because our AI is better at teaching. They pick us because after they come here, the kid starts speaking English on their own."

That sentence, woke me up. The brand core was not AI teaching. It was making kids want to learn.

That gap in understanding, was the starting point for the positioning.

Final positioning we settled on: Accent perfect. Global conversations. Your AI English expert. Slogan: Pronounce it right. Progress it sharp. Future it steady.

Once positioning locked in, the rest actually moved fast. Brand manual I did two versions. One full version, for the design team and vendors, everything there. One stripped version, ten pages total, for sales, marketing, ops — every page answering one question: How do I use this brand? Most brand manuals, after they get made, nobody but the designer reads them. I thought that was waste.

Website we only did three pages. Home. Product. Brand story. I made the prototypes myself, interaction and logic, designers only handled visual execution. Not chasing perfect. Chasing something we could ship, that people could understand.

Nine-level growth ladder, with exact learning expectations at each level, that also got locked in. Launch event full deck I wrote myself, from creative concept to visual system to the founder's core speech blocks. Core was one thing: make clear what the brand is, make clear what the advantage is. No stacked PPT fluff.

Thirty days later, we shipped. Brand system was not perfect. But it ran. The whole team, went from nobody knowing how to introduce ourselves, to everybody able to say exactly who we were.

The night after the launch event, I was alone in the office.

Most of the lights were off.

Only the desk lamp at my station, still on. The desktop was scattered with whiteboard markers, printed drafts of the brand manual, and a cup of coffee that had gone cold.

Tired? Tired. But those thirty days, had actually, helped me, figure out, one thing, for myself. A brand's core, is not the logo, is not the color palette. It is a complete system of knowing.

Knowing what to do.

Knowing what not to do. I couldn't have put it clearly then. But now, looking back, every stuck moment in those thirty days, was answering that question, for me, one by one.

Every meaningful thing usually starts with a small light somewhere.
